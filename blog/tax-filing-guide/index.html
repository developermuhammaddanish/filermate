Step-by-Step Guide to Filing Your Income Tax Return in Pakistan (2026)

Filing your income tax return is a legal obligation for every individual, salaried employee, freelancer, business owner, and corporate entity in Pakistan. This comprehensive guide walks you through everything you need to know for the tax year 2025-2026.

With the FBR mandating online filing for individual taxpayers, the process has become more streamlined but it still requires careful attention to detail. Whether you're a salaried professional, a freelancer, or a business owner, this guide will help you navigate the tax filing process with confidence.

What Is an Income Tax Return?

An income tax return is a declaration submitted to the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) that contains details of your earnings, applicable deductions, and taxes paid during a financial year. Filing timely returns helps you:

  • Avoid penalties and late fees
  • Become an active taxpayer (ATL benefits)
  • Meet visa and loan documentation requirements
  • Build credibility as a professional or business owner

💡 Key Point: The tax year in Pakistan runs from July 1 to June 30. The deadline for filing annual income tax returns is typically September 30 each year.

Why Filing Your Tax Return Matters in 2026

Stay Compliant with the Law

Tax laws are numerous and updated often, and it is easy for your business to fall out of compliance. Not meeting these requirements can result in big fines, audits, and perhaps legal consequences.

Access Financial Benefits

Once filed, your name enters the Active Taxpayers List (ATL) meaning reduced tax rates on banking transactions, vehicle/token registration, and property transfers. Non-filers pay significantly higher withholding tax rates.

Build Business Credibility

An active taxpayer status is increasingly required for business loans, visa applications, and working with corporate clients.

Avoid Penalties

If you register but fail to file an annual income tax return, your name is removed from the ATL. You lose all filer benefits and may face penalty notices from FBR.

Lower Withholding Tax Rates

Filers pay significantly lower tax rates on banking transactions, property purchases, and vehicle registrations compared to non-filers.

Business Credibility

Being on the ATL opens doors for business loans, corporate partnerships, and international opportunities.

Legal Protection

Regular filing protects you from penalties, audit notices, and legal complications with tax authorities.

Visa & Travel Benefits

Many countries require proof of tax compliance for visa applications. Being on the ATL simplifies this process.

Updated Tax Slabs for FY 2025-2026

Understanding the tax slabs helps you estimate your liability. Here are the updated rates for salaried individuals for the tax year 2025-2026:

Annual Income Range (PKR) Applicable Tax Rate
Up to 600,000 0%
600,001 – 1,200,000 2.5% of amount exceeding 600,000
1,200,001 – 2,200,000 PKR 6,000 + 11% of excess over 1,200,000
2,200,001 – 3,200,000 PKR 116,000 + 23% of excess over 2,200,000
3,200,001 – 4,100,000 PKR 346,000 + 30% of excess over 3,200,000
Above 4,100,000 PKR 616,000 + 35% of excess over 4,100,000

Note: For business owners and freelancers, different tax rates may apply. Business income is typically taxed at progressive rates ranging from 10% to 35% depending on the business structure and income level.

Step-by-Step Guide to Filing Your Tax Return

The entire process is now online through FBR's IRIS system at iris.fbr.gov.pk. Follow these steps carefully:

1

Register on the FBR IRIS Portal

If you do not already have an NTN, go to the FBR IRIS portal and click on "Registration for Unregistered Person." Enter your CNIC number and mobile number. You will receive a PIN via SMS to verify your identity and set up your login credentials.

2

Update Your Profile (Form 181)

Log into your IRIS account and complete or update your registration profile. This includes personal information, residential address, income source, and banking details. Make sure to select the correct business category (e.g., Freelancer / Service Provider / Business Owner / Salaried Individual).

3

Gather Required Documents

Before you begin filing, make sure you have all necessary documents ready:

  • Bank statements (showing all income sources, foreign remittances, or business revenue)
  • Withholding tax details (ATM withdrawals, profit on deposits, tax deducted at source)
  • Expense receipts (internet, software, utilities, laptop, rent, office supplies, etc.)
  • Donation/insurance receipts (if applicable)
  • Asset details (property, vehicles, investments)
  • Loan or liability details (if applicable)

Pro Tip for Freelancers: Your bank statements are enough to file your return if you do not own property or claim special credits. Keep them safe they are your strongest evidence of income.

4

File Return of Income (Form 114)

This is the main tax return form. You must declare all sources of income including:

  • Salary
  • Business profits and gains
  • Rental income
  • Capital gains
  • Other sources (dividends, bank interest, prize winnings, etc.)

For salaried persons, the Declaration form 114(I) is available. Salaried persons need to complete this declaration form to successfully submit their Income Tax Return.

5

File Wealth Statement (Form 116)

You must declare your assets, liabilities, and reconcile income vs. expenses. This is known as the Wealth Statement (Form 116).

⚠️ Important: Wealth Statement will only be successfully submitted once current year's wealth has increased/decreased from previous year's wealth by the same amount as your income has exceeded/fallen short of your expenses. Failure to reconcile wealth statement won't allow you to submit your Income Tax Return.

6

Generate PSID & Pay (if needed)

If you have any payable tax, the system will generate a PSID (Payment Slip ID). You can pay via online banking or at any National Bank of Pakistan branch.

7

Submit & Download Acknowledgment

Once you have completed both forms, submit them. Successful submission is confirmed when both the Return of Income and Wealth Statement forms have moved from the Draft folder to Completed Task. Download the acknowledgment for your records.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Filing Tax Returns

Missing the filing deadline

The due date for filing income tax returns is typically September 30 each year. Missing this deadline results in penalties and removal from the ATL.

Misclassifying income or expenses

Ensure you correctly categorize your income sources and claim only legitimate deductions. Incorrect classification can trigger audits and penalties.

Forgetting to reconcile wealth statement

As noted above, failure to reconcile the wealth statement will prevent you from submitting your return. This is one of the most common reasons for rejected filings.

Not claiming all legitimate deductions

Many businesses overlook deductions that could significantly reduce taxable income, including office rent, utilities, business travel, professional training, depreciation of equipment, and charitable donations to approved institutions.

Relying on outdated exemptions

Tax laws change frequently. Always use the current year's forms and rates. What was exempt last year may not be exempt this year.

Filing incomplete information

If you skip sections or leave fields blank, your return may be rejected or flagged for audit. Take the time to fill everything out completely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the deadline for filing income tax returns in Pakistan?

The deadline for filing annual income tax returns is typically September 30 each year.

Q: Do I need to file a return if my income is below the taxable limit?

If your annual income is below PKR 600,000, you may not owe any tax, but it is still recommended to file a nil return to maintain your Active Taxpayer List status and the benefits that come with it.

Q: What happens if I don't file a return after getting my NTN?

If you register but fail to file an annual income tax return, your name is removed from the ATL. You lose all filer benefits and may face penalty notices from FBR.

Q: Do freelancers who earn from abroad need to file a tax return?

Yes, even if you are earning from foreign clients and may be eligible for tax exemptions, you still need to be a registered filer and file an annual return to claim these exemptions legally.

Q: Is online filing mandatory now?

Yes. The FBR has mandated online submission of income tax returns and withholding statements for individual taxpayers under new income tax rules.

Q: What is the difference between Form 114 and Form 116?

Form 114 is the Return of Income form where you declare your income, expenses, and tax credits. Form 116 is the Wealth Statement where you declare assets, liabilities, and reconcile income vs. expenses.

Filing your income tax return is not just a legal requirement it is a smart financial decision that protects your interests and unlocks valuable benefits. In 2026, with FBR's focus on digital compliance and stricter enforcement, staying on the Active Taxpayer List is more important than ever.

The process is straightforward if you prepare your documents in advance and follow the steps carefully. And if you need help, professional tax consultants can guide you through the process, ensure accuracy, and help you claim every deduction you are entitled to.
